# Plain Language Research


## Steps
1. Find the one main finding and say it in one sentence.
2. Replace jargon with everyday words, or define the term in brackets.
3. Turn statistics into natural frequencies ("3 in 100 people" instead of "3%" or "RR 1.5").
4. Say how sure the researchers are and why.
5. Say what it means in practice and what it does not mean.

## Levels
- **Very simple:** short sentences, everyday examples, no numbers unless essential.
- **General public:** plain words, key numbers explained.
- **Professional:** accurate terms with brief explanations.

## Useful comparisons
Explain an effect size by comparing it with something familiar, for example "about the difference between an average and a slightly above-average score."

## Output format
**The short answer**, **What the researchers did**, **What they found**, **How sure we can be**, **What it means for you**, and **Words to know**.

## Rules
Never simplify a finding into something it is not ("linked to" must not become "causes").
