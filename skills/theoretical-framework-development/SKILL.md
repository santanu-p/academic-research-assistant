# Theoretical framework development

Build a framework that explains what the study proposes to examine and why its concepts and relationships are plausible. A framework may guide exploratory interpretation, formal modeling, or empirical prediction; do not force all three into one design.

## Choose the frame

Identify the phenomenon, question, disciplinary setting, scale, and intended use of the framework. Distinguish an established theory from a conceptual model assembled for the project and from a mathematical model that specifies equations. Locate and inspect relevant primary sources when their claims or terminology matter; record when access or search coverage is limited.

Compare plausible candidate frames on the phenomenon each addresses, central assumptions, constructs, mechanisms, level of analysis, and observable implications. Explain the choice in relation to the research question, including a serious alternative and why it was not selected or should be retained. Avoid selecting a popular label solely to decorate a proposal. If the literature has not been searched adequately, describe the choice as provisional rather than claiming novelty or field consensus.

## Make the argument explicit

Define each construct and distinguish it from its operational indicator. State relationships with their direction, conditions, and mechanism where supported. Mark whether each link is an established finding, a theoretical proposition, an assumption, or an open question. A diagram may make these links legible, but a diagram alone does not establish causality.

For formal or computational theories, give quantities, domains, dimensions, equations, initial or boundary conditions, approximations, and parameter meanings as relevant. For qualitative or interpretive work, explain how the lens directs attention and how participants' accounts can challenge it. Do not impose quantitative variables where the chosen approach does not require them.

Identify rival mechanisms, omitted constructs, boundary cases, and conditions under which the proposed relationship would fail or be difficult to distinguish from an alternative. Explain what observations or analysis would support, challenge, or revise the framework, including limitations of the intended study. Cite source ideas accurately and label new combinations as proposals rather than validated theories.

## Deliver

Provide a concise rationale, defined concepts, assumptions and relationships, evidence-linked propositions or interpretive questions, alternatives, and the connection to methods. Include a simple model diagram if it clarifies the argument. Record sources actually inspected, unresolved conceptual choices, and checks still needed. Do not claim that framework selection proves the hypothesis or establishes a causal effect.

Where the question and framework justify them, label proposed mediators and moderators in the relationship map, explain their different roles, and identify how each would be observed or investigated. Distinguish a hypothesized pathway or interaction from one established by evidence; do not add either solely to make the diagram more elaborate. Offer a concise, editable narrative explaining the selected frame, its fit to the question, and its principal limitation, with citations only to sources actually verified.
