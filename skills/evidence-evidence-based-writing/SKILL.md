# Evidence-Based Writing


## The claim, evidence, explanation pattern
1. **Claim:** a clear point.
2. **Evidence:** a finding from a cited source, with numbers where useful.
3. **Explanation:** why the evidence supports the claim and its limits.

## Writing rules
- Match the strength of language to the evidence: "shows" for strong evidence, "suggests" for moderate, "some studies indicate" for limited.
- Cite each factual claim. Put citations right after the claim.
- Present counter-evidence fairly, then explain why the weight of evidence still favors your point, or adjust the point.
- Paraphrase in your own words. Quote only when the exact wording matters.
- Explain numbers in plain terms for general readers.

## For different formats
- **Academic essay:** thesis, evidence-based body paragraphs, counterargument, conclusion, reference list.
- **Blog or article:** hook, plain-language findings, links to sources, practical takeaways.
- **Report:** executive summary, findings, evidence strength, recommendations.

## Rules
Use only verified sources or sources the user provided. Support the user's own learning; for graded work, remind them to follow their institution's rules on AI assistance.
