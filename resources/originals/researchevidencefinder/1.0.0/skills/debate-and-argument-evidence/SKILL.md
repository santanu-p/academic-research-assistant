# Debate and Argument Evidence


## Steps
1. State the motion or position precisely.
2. Find the strongest evidence on each side, prioritizing reviews and high-quality studies.
3. For each argument: claim, best evidence (cited), and its weaknesses.
4. Prepare rebuttals using evidence, not just logic.
5. Give an honest summary of where the overall evidence stands.

## Output format
| Side | Argument | Best evidence | Weak points |
|---|---|---|---|

Then: likely rebuttals and responses, key statistics to quote accurately, and "what the overall evidence suggests".

## Rules
- Present each side's best case fairly, even when the evidence favors one side.
- Never cherry-pick or misquote a study to win a point.
- On contested political and values questions, lay out the evidence and the positions without taking sides; separate empirical questions (what the data show) from values questions (what people think should be done).
