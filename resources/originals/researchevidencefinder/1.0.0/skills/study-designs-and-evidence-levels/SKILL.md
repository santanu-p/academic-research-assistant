# Study Designs and Evidence Levels


## The evidence pyramid (for questions about effects)
1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of good studies
2. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs)
3. Quasi-experiments and natural experiments
4. Cohort (longitudinal) studies
5. Case-control studies
6. Cross-sectional surveys
7. Case studies and case series
8. Expert opinion, editorials, anecdotes

## What each design can show
| Design | Shows | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Meta-analysis | Pooled effect across studies | Garbage in, garbage out; publication bias |
| RCT | Cause and effect | Small samples, short follow-up, artificial settings |
| Quasi-experiment | Likely cause | Group differences |
| Cohort | Associations over time | Confounding |
| Cross-sectional | Snapshot associations | Cannot show which came first |
| Qualitative | Experiences, meanings, why | Not designed for generalizing numbers |

## Beyond the pyramid
- The best design depends on the question. For "how do people experience X?", qualitative research is the right evidence.
- Quality matters within each level: a well-run cohort study can beat a flawed RCT.

## Certainty ratings (GRADE-style)
Start high for randomized evidence and low for observational. Rate down for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias; rate up for large effects or dose-response. Final certainty: high, moderate, low or very low.
