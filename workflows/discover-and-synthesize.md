# Discover and synthesize

Inputs: topic/question, discipline, date/scope limits and supplied sources. Read source files before defining the gap.

1. Use `scholar-research-question` and `scholar-theoretical-framework` to define answerable aims and a framework. With a one-line idea, `arms-brief-expander` can build a brief; proposals stay labelled proposals.
2. Read `evidence-database-guide`, `evidence-search-strings`, `evidence-find-studies` and `evidence-citation-chaining`. Execute available searches, recording database, query, date and inclusion logic. With no search capability deliver the queries and report that search was not executed.
3. Verify identity/DOI and retrieve lawful full text via `evidence-source-verification` and `evidence-open-access-full-text`. Extract claims against page/section locations with `scholar-paper-breakdown` and `evidence-data-extraction`.
4. Apply `evidence-study-appraisal`, `evidence-compare-studies`, `scholar-evidence-synthesis` and relevant `evidence-literature-review`/`scholar-literature-review`. Separate comparable results and methodological differences.
5. Use both research-gap specialists and `guardrails-research-problem-mechanism-evidence` to test a candidate gap against multiple sources; absence in a small search is not proof of novelty.

Outputs: search log, verified bibliography, evidence matrix, synthesis and bounded candidate gap/question. Gate: every material claim has a source or an explicit status as analysis/proposal. Optional role: `agents/evidence-librarian.md`.
