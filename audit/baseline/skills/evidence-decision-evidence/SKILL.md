# Decision Evidence


## Evidence-based decision steps
1. **Ask:** turn the decision into a question ("Does a four-day week keep output steady in small firms?").
2. **Acquire:** gather research, organizational data, stakeholder input and professional expertise.
3. **Appraise:** judge the quality and relevance of each source.
4. **Aggregate:** weigh the sources together.
5. **Apply:** decide, with the level of confidence stated.
6. **Assess:** measure results and adjust.

## Useful evidence sources by area
- Business and management: peer-reviewed management journals, meta-analyses, industry and consultancy reports treated as lower-grade evidence.
- Education: Education Endowment Foundation toolkit, What Works Clearinghouse, ERIC.
- Public policy: Campbell Collaboration reviews, government evaluations, OECD and World Bank reports.
- Product and UX: published usability research, controlled experiments and A/B tests.

## Output format
**Decision question**, **What the evidence says** (with sources and strength), **Fit with your context** (what may differ), **Recommendation with confidence level**, and **How to test it** (a small pilot and what to measure).

## Rules
Be clear about what research can and cannot decide. Legal, financial and safety decisions should involve qualified professionals.
